Your school’s mascot has been pulled into a four-level adventure across a wildly reimagined version of campus. To save the school’s spirit trophy, the mascot must race, jump, and outsmart obstacles—then face the rival school’s mascot in a final showdown.
Make it an original game inspired by classic side-scrolling platformers; use your own characters, art, names, and sounds rather than copying existing game assets.
Core requirements
    • One playable hero inspired by your school mascot. 
    • Four levels with a clear beginning, middle, and finish: 
        1. Campus Dash: Include at least two recognizable campus details, such as a library, mascot statue, quad, or dining hall. 
        2. Themed challenge: A new setting or obstacle type—perhaps a gym, underground tunnel, or stormy sports field. 
        3. The gauntlet: Combine challenges from earlier levels and introduce one new twist. 
        4. Rivalry showdown: End with a boss encounter against the rival school’s mascot. 
    • At least one collectible power-up that visibly changes what the hero can do. 
    • A way to win and a way to fail, with clear feedback to the player. 
    • A playable, testable build that another team can try.

# ACM Mid-Southeast 2026 Vibe Coding Competition

## Competition Rules

### Core Constraints

    • Time limit: Teams have 60 minutes from the official start to complete and submit their project. 
    • Code: All code in the final product must be generated by AI. Participants may write prompts and instructions for AI tools, but may not write or manually edit code themselves. 
    • Images and media: Visual assets must be AI-generated or sourced from the public domain. School logos are also permitted, including official logos that are not public domain. Use only a logo for a school represented in the competition, and do not alter it in a way that misrepresents the school. 
    • Publication: The final product must be published online and accessible to judges through a public URL by the end of the competition. 
    • Submission: Each team must submit its live URL before the deadline. 

### Judging Criteria

Each submission will be scored from 1 to 5 in each category, with 5 being the highest score.

    • Adherence to theme: How well does the product capture the intended goal? 
    • User experience: Is the product functional, engaging, and intuitive? 
    • Visual coherence: Do the visuals work well together and support the overall concept? 

### Disqualification Rules

A team may be disqualified for:
    • Including code that a participant wrote or manually edited in the final submission. 
    • Using visual assets that are neither AI-generated nor public domain, except for permitted school logos. 
    • Failing to make the product publicly accessible by the deadline. 
    • Reusing code or assets from a previous project. 

Note: The school-logo exception applies only to school logos. Other non-public-domain visual assets are not permitted unless they are AI-generated.
